## The thesis

**Governance is the product, not the paperwork.** Most AI governance stalls as
a policy nobody can operate. It earns its keep only when it ships as operating
models, controls and assurance built into the way AI actually gets made. That
is the same instinct I bring to product work: a principle isn't done until a
team can run it.

## What's on it

- **Perspectives.** Long-form writing on governance in practice, such as
  reading the EU AI Act as a delivery backlog, turning oversight principles
  into controls for AI agents, and why the field needs an academy more than
  another framework.
- **Initiatives**, each with its stage stated openly:
  - **[AI Governance Academy](/work/aigov-academy)** (live): a freemium
    learning platform that turns governance frameworks into decisions and
    controls people can run.
  - **GRC Academy** (pilot): governance, risk and compliance learned by doing,
    through realistic risk journeys. The first pilot is a Credit Risk
    "Lender's Journey".
  - **Governed AI agents** (shipped): guardian agents over critical data,
    language models reading metadata, and an orchestration layer, each with
    oversight and accountability patterns built in.
  - **Operating frameworks** (in practice): reusable tools such as asset
    tiering, embedded virtual teams and intake-to-assurance playbooks.

## Why it's a product, not a blog

- **One audience, one job.** It's written for the people who sit between
  legal, risk, engineering and product and have to make governed AI happen.
- **Writing and building feed each other.** Each essay argues for something an
  initiative then tries to make real, and the initiatives give the writing
  something concrete to point at.
- **Honest about maturity.** Every initiative carries its stage (live, pilot,
  shipped or in practice), so a reader knows what exists and what is still
  being proved.
- **A distinct identity.** A separate site with its own voice keeps the
  governance work focused, while this portfolio covers product craft more
  broadly.
