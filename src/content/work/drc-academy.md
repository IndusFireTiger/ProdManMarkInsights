## The problem

People who build data products meet compliance as a wall of acronyms. The
first question is always the same: *which of these actually apply to us?*
Answering it usually needs a lawyer, a consultant or weeks of reading. The
concepts underneath (the data lifecycle, the types of risk, the controls that
reduce them) are rarely explained in terms of what the product has to do.

## What it does

- **The Navigator.** Five questions (jurisdictions, sector, types of data,
  data activities, and whether you're a controller, a processor or both)
  produce a map of the laws, standards and obligations most likely to apply,
  with next steps.
- **Worked examples.** Four hypothetical products (a health SaaS, a fintech
  lender, a children's EdTech app and a startup in an uncovered jurisdiction)
  fill in the questions for you, so nobody starts from a blank form.
- **The DRC Road.** Six lessons, end to end: the data lifecycle, the hazards,
  compliance as a spec, the controls, governance and audit, and incident
  response. Each stop pairs a concept with what your product must do about it.
- **Policy Library and Atlas.** Sample policies mapped to the controls they
  establish, the obligations they help with and the risks they address, plus
  an atlas of the regulations behind them.

## Product choices worth noting

- **Start from the user's question, not the syllabus.** The front door is
  "what applies to me?", and the lessons come after it. People learn when they
  have a reason to.
- **Honest about its limits.** A clear notice says it's educational guidance,
  not legal advice, and not exhaustive. In a regulated domain, being trusted
  matters more than sounding complete.
- **No sign-in.** Progress is saved in the browser, which removes the biggest
  drop-off point for a free learning tool.
- **Written for builders.** Every concept ends in a product consequence,
  because that's the gap I saw from inside a DRC product team.
