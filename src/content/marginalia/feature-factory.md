## What it is

The failure mode where the roadmap becomes a production schedule. Velocity is
tracked, releases are celebrated, and the question of whether any of it moved a
customer outcome is asked by nobody in particular — because no role owns the
answer.

## Where people get it wrong

**Thinking it's caused by bad prioritisation.** It usually isn't. Feature
factories often prioritise carefully, with scoring frameworks and stakeholder
input. The defect is structural: the discovery unit is missing the person who
owns market context, so trade-offs get made without anyone in the room
representing the buyer's alternative. Fixing this is what the fourth seat in
the [synthetic quad](/marginalia/synthetic-product-quad) is for.

**Assuming it looks chaotic.** The mature version is orderly and busy. Sprints
close, burndowns look healthy, stakeholders are consulted. That's what makes it
durable — nothing in the operating metrics registers a problem.

**Believing AI helps.** When building gets dramatically cheaper, a feature
factory doesn't become a learning organisation — it becomes a faster feature
factory. Lowering the cost of production raises the value of judgement about what
deserves producing; that's the [operating-model
inversion](/perspectives/ai-product-operating-model), and it cuts both ways.

## How to check

One question, applied to the last two quarters: **what did we decide not to
build, and what evidence killed it?**

A roadmap with no cancellations isn't a sign of good picking. It's a sign that
nothing was ever really being decided.
