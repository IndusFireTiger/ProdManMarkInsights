Most gamification bolts extrinsic rewards onto content: points for completion,
streaks for showing up, badges for volume. It works briefly and generalises
badly, because the reward is detached from the thing being learned — and when
the novelty fades, so does the behaviour.

The Seeker → Sage spine borrows the structure of the tradition it teaches: named
stages of understanding, each one a recognisable identity rather than a number.
The learner isn't accumulating points; they're moving through a sequence that
means something inside the subject matter itself.

Two design consequences follow. Progress has to be earned through demonstrated
understanding rather than time served, or the identity is hollow. And the stages
have to be *nameable by the learner* — a child should be able to say which one
they are and what the next one requires.

Gamification in service of learning, rather than gamification pointed at
engagement and hoping learning follows.
