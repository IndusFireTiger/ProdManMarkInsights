Most internal change is delivered as a memo: a decision announced, a deadline
attached, compliance assumed. It produces the appearance of adoption — people do
the minimum visible thing — and reverts the moment attention moves elsewhere.

The alternative treats the change exactly like a product going to market. It gets
a name people can say. It gets a [value
proposition](/marginalia/positioning) aimed at what the *adopter* gains, not what
the organisation gains. It gets early adopters chosen for willingness rather than
seniority, whose results become the proof for everyone else. And it gets
supporting material, because a change nobody can explain to a colleague doesn't
propagate.

The underlying insight is that internal audiences are audiences. They have
alternatives — chiefly ignoring you, the internal version of [the institutional
"no"](/marginalia/institutional-no) — and they respond to evidence from peers far
more than to instruction from above.

Coined for a campaign to move an engineering organisation off waterfall, where
the mandate had already been tried and had already failed.
