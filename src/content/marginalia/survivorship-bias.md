## What it is

A sub-species of [selection bias](/marginalia/selection-bias) with a particular
mechanism: the filter is *survival*. You study the funds that still exist, the
founders who made it, the customers who renewed — and quietly treat their shared
traits as causes of success, when the same traits are just as likely present in
everyone who failed and disappeared.

## Where people get it wrong

Two habits, both common in product work.

**Learning from winners only.** Case studies, "how we grew to $10M," and
best-practice decks are compiled entirely from survivors. They can tell you what
successful companies did; they cannot tell you whether unsuccessful companies did
the same thing, which is the only comparison that would make it evidence.

**Treating current users as the market.** Your active users are the ones your
onboarding didn't lose. Optimising further for them looks like listening to
customers and often means sanding the product ever more precisely to the
preferences of the people who were already fine — while the actual growth
constraint sits with people who left before you ever measured them.

## How to check

Name the missing population out loud before drawing the conclusion: *who left,
and would they be in this data?* If the answer is no, the finding is about
survivors and should be stated that way.

Where it matters commercially, go find them — churn interviews, failed-trial
outreach, lost-deal reviews. The absent data is usually recoverable; it's just
nobody's job to recover it.
