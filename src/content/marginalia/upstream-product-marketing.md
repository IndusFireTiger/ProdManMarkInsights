## What it is

A question of *when*, not of skill. The same person doing the same craft produces
something entirely different depending on whether they arrive before the
trade-offs or after them. Upstream, positioning is a constraint on scope.
Downstream, it's a description of whatever emerged.

## Where people get it wrong

**Treating it as an advocacy problem.** Almost everyone agrees product marketing
should be involved earlier. Agreement has never been the constraint — capacity
is. When roughly 44% of product marketing teams are one or two people and a third
of practitioners carry five or more products, "be in discovery for all of them"
isn't a behaviour anyone declined to adopt. It's arithmetic.

**Prescribing seniority instead of structure.** Hiring a more senior PMM into a
1:5 ratio produces a more senior person doing downstream work. What changes the
outcome is a gate — a positioning artifact required before budget is allocated —
plus enough capacity that holding the gate is survivable.

**Automating the wrong half.** Making launch collateral cheaper to produce
relieves the visible pain without moving anyone upstream, and quietly reduces the
pressure to. See [launch factory](/marginalia/launch-factory).

## How to check

Six-month test, and it isn't a dashboard: **did anything get cancelled?**

If upstream work never killed a feature, redirected an epic or changed a price
before launch, product marketing is still downstream regardless of when the
meetings happen.
