The product trio — product manager, designer, engineering lead — balances value,
usability and feasibility, and has a hole exactly where distribution, market
context and commercialisation should sit. The trio decides; the product marketer
interprets afterwards.

The standard fix is to add a fourth person, which is a hiring request arriving at
an executive who, in a plurality of companies, doesn't fund the function at all.
The synthetic version fills the seat differently: a human keeps the judgement and
the decision rights, while agents absorb the labour — win/loss trawling,
competitor diffing, first-draft artifacts — that made the seat unaffordable.

"Synthetic" is doing honest work in that phrase. It isn't a fourth colleague. It
is one person with enough leverage to behave like one, which is a weaker claim
than the org-design literature makes and a considerably more achievable one.
