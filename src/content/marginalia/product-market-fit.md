## What it is

A relationship, not a property. Fit exists between a specific product and a
specific market at a specific moment — which means it can be lost without the
product changing at all, because the other half of the relationship moved.

## Where people get it wrong

**Treating it as a milestone.** The language encourages it: teams talk about
"achieving PMF" as though it were a gate you pass through on the way to scaling.
That framing licenses a dangerous shift in attention, where discovery gets
wound down precisely when the market is starting to move underneath you. Fit is
perishable — see [PMF decay](/marginalia/pmf-decay).

**Reading growth as proof.** Growth confirms fit only while the acquisition
channel and the market both hold still. Plenty of products have grown for a year
on a channel advantage while their underlying fit was eroding.

**Confusing usage with fit.** People use things they'd abandon instantly for a
better option. Fit is better tested by what customers would do if you disappeared
— which is a question about their [competitive
alternatives](/marginalia/competitive-alternative), not your engagement charts.

## How to check

The cheap version of Sean Ellis's test still works: what share of users would be
very disappointed if the product vanished? But pair it with the harder question —
*what would they use instead, and how much worse is it really?* If the honest
answer is "something almost as good," you have usage, not fit.
