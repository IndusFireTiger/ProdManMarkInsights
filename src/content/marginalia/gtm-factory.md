A factory works because inputs are standardised, the stations are known, and the
line moves at a predictable pace. Go-to-market can work the same way once you
stop treating every launch as a blank page.

Three parts hold it together: [asset tiering](/marginalia/asset-tiering) so
launch weight matches launch significance; a virtual team reaching into
engineering so the story is sourced where the truth lives; and a cadence tied to
the release train, making marketing readiness a release gate rather than an
afterthought.

The point isn't to remove craft. It's to standardise the predictable so craft can
be spent where originality actually moves the market — and so the next unit costs
less than the last.

Not to be confused with its failure mode, the
[launch factory](/marginalia/launch-factory), which industrialises the same
downstream work without ever moving anyone upstream.
