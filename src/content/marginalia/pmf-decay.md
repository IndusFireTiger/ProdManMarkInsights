## What it is

Fit degrades through four distinct mechanisms, and they call for different
responses:

- **Market saturation** — the addressable buyers are captured; who's left wants
  it less or is locked to someone else.
- **Product saturation** — the core use case is genuinely solved, so further
  features return less and less.
- **Value decay** — macro shifts, new technology or changed expectations make
  yesterday's differentiator ordinary.
- **The [Red Queen effect](/marginalia/red-queen-effect)** — enormous
  engineering spend goes into holding position rather than gaining any.

## Where people get it wrong

**Waiting for a growth stall to investigate.** By the time the curve visibly
bends, the decay has been running for several quarters and the cheap responses
have expired. Decay is a leading-indicator problem being managed with lagging
indicators.

**Reading healthy aggregate numbers as health.** Total revenue and total users
keep climbing through early decay because the installed base masks it. The
signals move first in cohorts: activation among *new* users, time to first value
for *recent* signups, adoption of features shipped *this* year.

**Responding with more features.** The instinct when growth slows is to ship
harder, which is precisely the Red Queen trap and typically deepens the
complexity that's dampening broad adoption.

## How to check

Audit fit on a calendar, not on a scare — twice a year, cohort by cohort. Ask
whether a customer acquired this quarter reaches value as fast as one acquired
two years ago. If not, fit is decaying regardless of what the totals say.

The three exits are repositioning to an adjacent segment, adding complementary
value to the installed base, or re-purposing the underlying capability for a new
problem entirely.
