## What it is

Three documents produced *before* build: a mock press release written as though
the product already shipped, a customer-facing FAQ, and an internal FAQ covering
the awkward questions. Amazon's "Working Backwards" method.

The mechanism is forcing function. A press release nobody would want to read is a
product nobody wants, and that's discoverable in an afternoon rather than two
quarters. It also compels early agreement on the things teams otherwise defer —
who this is for, what it replaces, what it costs, what success looks like.

## The naming

**PR is press release.** Not public relations, though the acronym invites the
misreading and the artifact does resemble a PR deliverable. The distinction
matters because reading it as a communications exercise gets it delegated to
marketing after the fact — which inverts the entire point of writing it first.

## Where people get it wrong

Writing it after the roadmap is set. At that stage it becomes documentation of a
decision rather than a test of one, and the internal FAQ — the section carrying
most of the value, because it's where genuine objections live — quietly turns
into a list of objections already overruled.
